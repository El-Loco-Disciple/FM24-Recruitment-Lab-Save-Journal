Start here

Recruitment Lab turns a Football Manager player-search export into a recruitment department: role scores for every player, a Director of Football board, a shadow squad, club comparisons and one-page scouting reports. It works entirely in your browser. Nothing you import is uploaded anywhere.

Five-minute quick start

  1. Add the view to FM. Copy the view file from the download into FM’s views folder and import it on the Player Search screen. See Set up Football Manager.
  2. Export your world. In Player Search, with the view on and no filters, select every player and print the list to a web page (.html).
  3. Import it here. Choose Import season in the sidebar, pick the file, type the season as it appears in your save (written yyyy/yy, however many seasons in you are) and say how far through the season you are.
  4. Run the setup. Pick your league and club, a starting tactic and how you want to play. The Lab builds your best XI and finds your squad gaps.
  5. Open Home. Your weakest slot, the DoF picks for it, your contracts and prospects are waiting. Click any player to open his page.

Download a backup from Settings now and then. Your data lives in this browser on this device, and clearing the browser’s site data would wipe it.

Set up Football Manager

Install the view

  1. Find FM’s user folder: Documents / Sports Interactive / Football Manager 20XX (the year of your game).
  2. Open or create a folder called views inside it, and copy the .fmf view file from the download into it.
  3. In a save, go to Scouting → Players → Player Search (or press the search shortcut).
  4. Open the view menu at the top of the list, choose Custom → Import View, and pick the file. It now appears under your custom views.

The view is almost entirely the free FM Data Lab view, with one or two columns added. It contains every column the Lab reads: minutes, appearances, xG, xA, passing, pressing, defending, aerial and physical stats, plus wage, value, contract and personality. If you use your own view, it must include at least Name, Position, Club, Division, Age and Mins; anything else missing just switches off the stats that need it.

Export a season

  1. In Player Search, select the view and clear any filters, so the list covers every player in the leagues you loaded. Include your own club’s players: the Lab finds your squad from the same export.
  2. Select every row (Ctrl+A on Windows, Cmd+A on Mac).
  3. Print the screen (Ctrl+P or Cmd+P), choose Web Page, and save the .html file somewhere easy to find.

Exports with tens of thousands of players work, but they take longer to import. Players your scouts haven’t seen may be missing from FM’s search, depending on your game settings.

When to export

Once at the end of each season (around May) gives the cleanest data. A mid-season export in January is useful for the winter window: import it with the same season label and set season progress to half, then import again in May. The January import is kept for progress tracking.

Importing seasons

  • Season label. Type the season as it appears in your save, written yyyy/yy. Nothing is filled in for you, so it works ten seasons into a save as well as one. Seasons are ordered by label, so Risers and the ▲▼ arrows compare each season with the one before.
  • Season progress. Full, three-quarters, half or quarter. The Lab suggests one from the minutes in the file. A part-season import scales the minute floor, confidence bars and small-sample shrinkage so half-season numbers are judged fairly.
  • Importing the same season again replaces it, so you always work with the latest numbers. The earlier import is kept for progress tracking, and Settings can delete those earlier copies to free space.
  • Appearances. FM writes appearances as 25 (6): starts, then substitute appearances. The Lab counts both, so that is 31 appearances, 6 off the bench.
  • Leagues with limited stats. If an advanced stat such as xG or pressures is blank or zero for every player in a league, it’s treated as missing there rather than as real zeros. The Leagues page lists which leagues are affected.
  • Several saves. Each save keeps its own seasons, shortlist, tactics, budget and settings. Switch saves from the club box at the top of the sidebar. Your philosophy is shared by every save on this device.

Your club, tactic and philosophy

Setup

After the first import the Lab asks for your league and club, a starting tactic and how you want to play. You can run it again from Settings, and change everything later.

Tactic & best XI

Pick a formation and a role for every slot, or choose Create my own in the setup. Click a player on the pitch to choose who plays in that slot; placed players get a pin and stay put. The Lab picks the best XI from your squad, each player used once, and compares every slot with the median club in your league (each club gets its own best XI in your tactic). Save as many tactics as you like. Home, DoF picks, the Shadow squad and the Club finder all follow the tactic you choose here.

Philosophy score

  • Six principles: keep the ball, always available, move it to hurt, win it back in five, defend on the front foot and intensity. Each one averages a player’s percentiles in a few stats against players in the role’s position group. You can add or remove stats for each principle.
  • Weights by role: each role weights the six from 0 (not counted) to 3 (matters most). Click the dots to change them. A false nine might weight keeping the ball heavily and ignore front-foot defending.
  • Templates: nine play styles (Possession, Counter-press, Direct, Balanced, Low block and counter, Wing play, Positional play, High-intensity press and Physical) set every role’s weights in one go. Start from one, then adjust.
  • Bands: 70 and above fits the idea, 50 to 69 is coachable, under 50 is against the idea. If less than 60% of the weight can be measured, there’s no score.
  • Search positions: each role searches its own position plus any you add, for example a defensive midfielder for an inverted wing-back. Players from an added position are tagged Retrain. Their numbers come from the position they actually play, so treat a retrain score as a lead to scout.
  • Character test: reads the Personality column. Model citizens, professionals, resolute, determined and driven players pass; temperamental, volatile, casual, slack, unambitious and similar fail. Intelligence and technique are attributes, which the export doesn’t include.

A tour of the pages

Home

Your recruitment overview: best XI score against the league, your weakest slot, shortlist pipeline, contracts ending, saved searches, prospects and the DoF picks for the weakest slot. After a new import, a strip shows what changed for players on your lists.

Explore

  • Role search: pick a role, plot its two key stats, and rank everyone by role score. Filter by age, value, wage, contract, league, level, height, foot, nationality, minutes and stat conditions. Click any row to open the player.
  • Profile search: describe the player you want as index targets (must-haves and nice-to-haves) and rank by fit.
  • Prospects: young players doing well in limited minutes, ranked against their own age group.
  • Risers: players whose score rose most since last season, each season measured against its own pool.
  • Saved searches remember who matched. After each import they show how many players are new.
  • Options: adjust attacking output for league strength (the pill in the top bar), shrink small samples toward the median, and leave out defenders when ranking midfielders.

DoF picks

Ten players for one slot of your tactic, in five columns of two:

  • Dream: the best score in a stronger league that fits your budget and won’t refuse.
  • Upgrade: 10+ points better than your starter, aged 22 to 29, costing up to half the budget.
  • Value: the most score per €M, cheap and with medium or high confidence.
  • Squad player: rotation level, proven at your level or above.
  • Prospect: 21 or under, ranked against his age group.

Set your transfer budget and wage cap; they’re saved with each save. Switches leave out players likely to refuse and players against your philosophy, and players not for sale stay out unless you include them. Players who fail the character test are left out too; change that on the Philosophy score page. More filters adds age, how far above your league his club is, confidence and contract length. The footnote names the best players left out and why.

Scatter

Plot any two stats, role scores or custom indices for a position group. Drag a box to select players, add them to your shortlist, or compare the top four. Build your own indices from up to three stats.

Compare

Up to four players, always shown as A (blue), B (orange), C (green) and D (purple). Profile lanes, pizza charts, role fit and where they sit against the pool. Export the profile as an image.

Shortlist and Shadow squad

Two lists per save: first team and prospects. Move players through Watching, Scouted, Bid and Signed (or Rejected) and keep notes. Shadow squad places your first-team shortlist into your tactic: each player goes to the slot where he scores best among the positions he can play, and Move puts him anywhere. Gold is your starter; a slot where nobody on your list beats him links to DoF picks. The strip at the top adds up the cost of the top pick in each slot you’d upgrade.

Leagues and Benchmarks

Leagues shows each league’s rating, size and any limited stats. Benchmarks shows how a role scores across every league, so you can judge whether a player’s numbers will travel.

Club finder

For when you want to move. Every club’s best XI in your tactic, ranked by Foundation: 45% tactical fit, 35% philosophy and 20% depth (the second-best player in each slot). Filter by top 5 or top 10 leagues, region, league level, most gaps (5 by default), what counts as a gap, XI age and more. Preview as this club shows Home, DoF picks and the Shadow squad as that club, with a budget estimated from its squad, without changing your save.

Player page

Click any name. Assess as switches the whole profile to a role: what the role needs, your philosophy principles weighted for it, and anything else that stands out. You also get role fit with philosophy scores, roles he could retrain into, indices with changes on last season, similar players, his pipeline status and your notes. The copy button next to a name copies it for FM’s search.

Scouting report

Export report on a player page builds a one-page report for the role you’re assessing him in: facts, a data verdict, percentile profile, where he sits, role fit, a comparison with your player in that role, indices and your notes. Download it as a web page, then open it and print to PDF if you need one.

Tracking progress

  • On the player page, Progress shows his score in the role you’re assessing at every import you’ve kept, for example 31/32 ½ 74, 31/32 79, 32/33 ½ 83.
  • On the shortlist, each player shows what he scored when you added him and the change since.
  • On Home, after a new import, Since your last import lists players on your lists who moved club, rose or fell by 5 or more, whose contract now ends within a year, or whose value changed by a quarter or more. Mark as seen clears it.

Backups, phones and installing

  • Where your data lives: in this browser on this device. It stays when you close the page, but clearing the browser’s site data, or using a private window, loses it. The sidebar says Saved on this device.
  • Backups: Settings → Download backup saves everything for the current save, including earlier imports, as one file. Home reminds you if it’s been more than two weeks. Load a backup or phone file brings a save back on any device.
  • Send to phone: Settings → Send to phone makes a smaller file with your two latest seasons, shortlist, tactics and philosophy. Move it to your phone (AirDrop, email, a cloud drive or a message to yourself), open the Lab’s link on the phone and choose Load a backup or phone file. Send it again after each import; the phone offers to replace the old copy.
  • Install as an app: in Chrome or Edge on a computer, use Install app in the sidebar or the install icon in the address bar. On Android, open the browser menu and choose Install app or Add to Home screen. On iPhone or iPad, open the Lab in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. It then opens full-screen and works without a connection.

Troubleshooting

  • “No Name column” on import: the file isn’t a Player Search export saved as a web page. Print the list again and choose Web Page.
  • Your club isn’t in the list during setup: the export didn’t include your league or your own players. Clear the search filters in FM and export again.
  • A player has no role score: fewer than 60% of the role’s stats could be measured, or he’s under the minutes floor. Small leagues with limited stats are the usual cause.
  • Scores look too high in a weak league: switch on the league adjustment in the top bar, and use the level filters. A number earned two levels below yours rarely travels intact.
  • Retrain scores look odd: they compare him with players who already play that position, using numbers from the position he actually plays. Treat them as a lead to scout.
  • Storage is full: delete old seasons or earlier imports in Settings, or old saves you no longer use. A season of around 9,000 players takes about 5 MB.
  • Everything disappeared: the browser’s site data was cleared, or you’re in a different browser or a private window. Load your latest backup.

Career Journal

  • A companion notebook. The Career Journal (Career Journal at the bottom of the sidebar, or journal.html) keeps the story of each save: season objectives, a tactic builder, transfer plans and the ledger, depth charts, tactical notes, a season review and screenshots.
  • Works on its own. It doesn’t need an export, has its own storage and backups, and installs as its own app on a computer or phone.
  • Export a season as one page to keep, share or print to PDF. JOURNAL.txt in the download has the full guide.

Before you plot

  • Define the role, not the position. A right-back can be a full-back, wing-back, inverted wing-back or wide centre-back, and each needs different evidence.
  • Use the same FM view every season. The library joins seasons by player ID, so a changed view breaks the history.
  • Per-90 for comparison, totals for context. Totals reward minutes, not quality.
  • Ratios need volume. Every percentage and calculated ratio has a minimum-attempts floor. Below it the stat is left blank rather than ranked.
  • Team style moves every number. Possession sides inflate passing volume and deflate defensive volume. Compare like with like where you can.
  • League strength doesn't translate one to one. Use the level filter, the league table and, if you want, the adjusted view.
  • The stats tell you what happened, not why. Treat a high score as a reason to look closer, not a signing.

Role scores

  • What it is. A weighted average of the role's key stats, each converted to a percentile against every player listed in that position who played 900+ minutes, across all leagues in the season.
  • Why all leagues. It keeps a score of 60 meaning the same thing everywhere, so benchmarks and best XIs are comparable. It also means players in weaker leagues score higher than they would against tougher opposition; use the level band and the adjusted view to account for it.
  • Duties change the weights. Defend duties lean towards defensive stats, attack duties towards shooting and creation, stopper towards tackling and pressing, cover towards reading the game. Automatic is the average of defend, support and attack.
  • Some duties score close together. Stats can't see positioning, so a centre-back on stopper and on cover will often produce similar numbers. The tool doesn't invent a difference that isn't in the data.
  • Missing data. If less than 60% of a role's weight can be measured for a player, he gets no score rather than a misleading one.

Pizza charts

  • Each slice is a percentile. 80 means ahead of 80% of the comparison pool. Raw values are in the table underneath.
  • Templates follow the position group. Every centre-back is drawn on the same fifteen stats so any two can be compared; the role lens marks the key stats with a white dot. Keepers get nine.
  • The pool decides the picture. Big five is the FBref standard and the toughest test. Always read the line above the chart first.

Best XIs and benchmarks

  • Best XI. For your tactic, the combination of players that maximises total role score, with each player used once and only in positions he's listed at. Your squad, any league, or a comparison group of clubs you choose.
  • Moving players. On your squad's best XI, click a player to pick who plays in that slot: anyone in your squad, in or out of position. A player from another slot swaps places. Players you place get a pin and stay put; the Lab fills the other slots around them. Placements are kept for each tactic and carry through to Home, DoF picks and the Shadow squad. Reset to the best XI clears them.
  • Slot benchmarks. Each club in the benchmark league or group gets its own best XI, and the table shows the median score and key stats of the player each club fields in every slot. That's "what a top-half side's striker actually produced", set against yours.
  • Competition benchmarks. The median role score and key stats of regular starters in each league, with your best player in that role measured against every league's median.
  • Comparison groups are saved with each save, because the clubs that matter change with your goal and your league.

League levels and the adjusted view

  • Levels use the game's own reputation stars for Europe, the Americas, Africa and Asia, compared with your league. Each half-star is one band.
  • The adjusted view is an assumption. It scales attacking output by a fixed rate per half-star, up for stronger leagues and down for weaker ones, then recalculates every percentile and role score. Defensive, physical and percentage stats are never adjusted.
  • Use it as a stress test. A player who stays near the top at 0% and at 20% doesn't depend on the assumption.
  • Coming with more seasons: once your library holds several seasons, players who move between leagues in your save can replace the assumed rate with a measured one.

Data health

  • Dist/90 exports as 0.0km for every player. Replaced by Dist/90 km, rebuilt from total Distance.
  • Sv % and xSv % come out negative. Replaced by Save %, rebuilt from saves and goals conceded.
  • Mins/Gl is 0.00 or blank and is left out.
  • xG-OP is goals minus expected goals, not open-play xG.
  • Any column with no variation in a season is hidden from the stat menus automatically.

Indices and search

  • Indices. Each index is the average of two or three stats, each as a percentile against players in the same position with 900+ minutes. Chance creation: xA, open-play key passes, chances created. Ball security: pass completion, loss rate, passes per loss. Ball progression: progressive passes, dribbles, verticality. Shot threat: npxG, shots, shots on target. Finishing: goals above xG, conversion, goals per shot on target. Ball winning: tackles won, interceptions, possession won. Pressing: pressures attempted and completed, pressure success. Engine: distance and sprints. Aerial: headers won, header win rate, key headers. Box defending: blocks, clearances and shots blocked. Discipline: cards, fouls and errors (fewer is better). Crossing: open-play crosses completed, open-play cross rate, crosses completed. Shot stopping: goals prevented, save rate, goals conceded. Reliability: start rate, minutes per appearance, average rating.
  • Finishing is luck-prone. Goals above xG swing a lot from season to season. Treat a high Finishing index as a reason to check the next season, not a reason to pay more.
  • Profile search. A must-have is a hard minimum. A nice-to-have only moves players up the ranking. Fit is the average of each index against its target, capped at 100. Balanced only drops anyone with any single component below 40.
  • Confidence. Three bars at 2,000+ minutes, two at 1,200+, one below. Shrink small samples pulls a score toward 50 in proportion to how far under 2,000 minutes he is.
  • Season progress. Set it when you import, or later in Settings. A part-season import scales the minute thresholds: three-quarters uses a 675-minute pool floor and 900 / 1,500 for confidence and shrinkage; half uses 600 and 600 / 1,000; quarter uses 600 and 300 / 500. The pool floor never drops below 600. Minute filters still on the old default move with it.
  • Prospects. A separate Explore mode for young players with few minutes. It defaults to 21 or under with 300+ minutes (scaled by season progress, never below 150). The age-group score ranks every stat against players his age in the same position; vs everyone uses the normal pool. Kinder shrinkage pulls a small sample toward the typical player his age instead of toward 50. Productive in limited minutes means an age-group score of 70+ on under half of the league minutes available, and Mostly off the bench flags players with at least as many substitute appearances as starts, where per-90 numbers often run high. For seasons imported before substitute appearances were read, they are estimated from minutes his starts don't explain (about 80 minutes a start, 22 a substitute appearance). Prospects you follow go in their own list on the Shortlist page.
  • Appearances. FM exports appearances as starts with substitute appearances in brackets, for example 25 (6). The Lab counts both, so 25 (6) is 31 appearances, 6 of them off the bench.
  • Leagues with limited stats. When an advanced stat such as xG, key passes or pressures is zero or blank for every player in a league, the Lab treats it as missing for that league. The Leagues page lists which leagues and stats are affected.
  • Likely to refuse means he plays in a stronger league than yours and has an ambitious or driven personality. It is a rough guide, not the game's own logic.
  • Risers compare each player's score this season with last season, each measured against its own season's pool, so league-wide changes cancel out.
  • Saved searches remember who matched when you last opened them. After you import a new season, the count of new matches shows next to each one.

Sweeper keeperStarts the attacks and covers the space behind a high line. Shot-stopping still …

Starts the attacks and covers the space behind a high line. Shot-stopping still decides whether he plays.

X axis Pas %

Keepers range from roughly 45% to 90% in this export, so unlike outfield players it genuinely separates the ones who can play from the ones who kick.

Y axis xGP/90

Goals prevented against expectation. Above zero means he is saving shots an average keeper concedes.

Target zone. Top right is secure in possession and saving goals. Bottom right is the trap: tidy with his feet and costing you goals. Never sign from the bottom half of this chart, however good the passing looks.

Filter first

  • Mins of 1,500 or more. One bad afternoon swings xGP/90 on a small sample, and keepers need the bigger one.
  • Ps A/90 of 20 or more. A 90% pass rate on eight short passes a game tells you nothing about distribution.
  • Age 22 to 31 for a starter. Keepers peak late, so a 29-year-old is not a short-term buy here.
  • Height 6'1" and up if you want him claiming crosses behind a high line.

Then check

  • Pr passes/90. Whether he breaks a line or only rolls it to the centre-backs. The top third of keepers here sits above about 0.9.
  • Int/90 and Poss Won/90. The closest the export gets to sweeping. Above about 0.3 interceptions per 90, the top tenth of keepers here, is one who reads balls in behind.
  • Gl Mst. More than two in a season is a pattern, not bad luck.
  • Con/90 and Clean Sheets for the defence in front of him, not as a measure of the keeper.

Red flags

  • Sv % and xSv % come out corrupted in this export, with negative values for every keeper. Ignore both and work from xGP/90.
  • A strong xGP/90 behind a deep-block side. He faces more shots, so has more chances to bank prevented goals. Check whether it holds at a club that dominates the ball.

Progression from deep. The libero exists to break the first line, so this is the primary measure.

  • Hdr % and Aer A/90. He is still a centre-back at set pieces.
  • Sprints/90 for recovery pace when the press breaks.

  • Ps A/90 inflated by playing for the dominant side in the league. Compare only against centre-backs at similar clubs.
  • High Poss Lost/90 alongside modest Pr passes/90. He is losing it without moving it forward.
